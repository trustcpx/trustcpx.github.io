<section id="overview" class="review-section">
  <p class="eyebrow">Cooperative perception · object-level sharing</p>
  <h2>See beyond one viewpoint.<br />Trust the evidence before fusion.</h2>
  <p class="lead-copy">Sharing detections helps robots overcome occlusions and limited sensing range. But an honest agent can be wrong, a message can be stale, and a malicious sender can fabricate objects. TrustCPX connects <strong>trust in each detection</strong> with <strong>trust in its source over time</strong> to decide which reports influence the shared scene.</p>

    </figure>
  </Wide>
  <p class="contribution-line"><strong>The contribution:</strong> a decentralized two-stage framework that combines 14 local trust factors with persistent peer reputation, then separates evidence-based judging from admission and reputation-weighted output fusion. TrustCPX is also deployed live at an instrumented intersection, fusing detections from infrastructure lidar and vehicle lidar online.</p>

<section id="method" class="review-section">
  <p class="eyebrow">02 / Method · local stage</p>
  <h2>First, assess your own detections.</h2>
  <p>Each agent detects and tracks objects, checks 14 complementary factors, and combines them into a <span class="term" tabindex="0">local trust score<span class="term-tip">A score from 0 to 1 summarizing how strongly the agent's own perception supports one tracked detection.</span></span>. Only tracked objects and their scores are shared; peers do not need access to the sender's raw sensor data.</p>

</section>

<section id="global-trust" class="review-section">
  <p class="eyebrow">03 / Method · global stage</p>
  <h2>Then, decide whose reports can contribute.</h2>
  <p>Every receiver keeps its own <span class="term" tabindex="0">peer reputation<span class="term-tip">A persistent score for a sender, updated from evidence observed by this receiver and admitted peers. It is not a single network-wide score.</span></span>. Corroborated reports earn credit; contradicted reports accumulate incorrect evidence. Freshness and volatility affect the admission gate without overwriting stored reputation.</p>
  <TrustPipeline />
  <div class="threat-overview">
    <h3>Different failure modes, different safeguards.</h3>
    <ul class="threat-paths">
      <li><strong>Fabricated objects</strong><span aria-hidden="true">→</span><span>Visible contradiction</span><span aria-hidden="true">→</span><span>Withhold reports and charge incorrect evidence</span></li>
      <li><strong>Stale messages</strong><span aria-hidden="true">→</span><span>Blind travel distance grows</span><span aria-hidden="true">→</span><span>Freshness lowers the admission score</span></li>
      <li><strong>Repeated drops</strong><span aria-hidden="true">→</span><span>Volatility retains drop history</span><span aria-hidden="true">→</span><span>Admission remains more cautious after rebounds</span></li>
      <li><strong>Newcomer collusion</strong><span aria-hidden="true">→</span><span>Only admitted peers can corroborate</span><span aria-hidden="true">→</span><span>Gated newcomers cannot vouch for each other</span></li>
    </ul>
  </div>
  <div class="metric-cards global-metrics">
    <div class="metric-card"><span>Honest control · reports fused</span><strong>99.6%</strong><p>Honest reports retained despite partial viewpoints.</p></div>
    <div class="metric-card"><span>Persistent-ghost sender · reports fused</span><strong>37.3%</strong><p>62.7% withheld; the sender fails admission in 52.5% of frames.</p></div>
  </div>
  <p class="chart-reading">Five-agent simulation. Percentages describe all reports from the tested sender, including genuine detections. They are not malicious-actor detection rates or ghost-only rejection rates.</p>
  <details class="research-details">
    <summary>Why a missing corroboration does not automatically mean “false”</summary>
    <p>A peer may see an object that ego cannot. Unverified reports are held without a charge; a contradiction requires contemporaneous visibility evidence. Below reputation 0.95, an admitted sender's reports require corroboration. At or above 0.95, the broader admission tier still withholds reports currently contradicted or charged by the ledger.</p>
    <p>Held-only frames leave reputation unchanged. Corroboration can settle a held report later. The update affects the next frame; admission never blocks future judging.</p>
  </details>

<section id="results" class="review-section">
  <p class="eyebrow">04 / Evaluation</p>
  <h2>Three questions. Three kinds of evidence.</h2>
  <article class="result-study">
    <span class="study-label">1 / Score quality · real-world validation</span>
    <h3>Do combined factors rank detections better?</h3>
    <p>The learned local trust function reaches <strong>0.778 mAP</strong>, versus <strong>0.647</strong> for model confidence alone. The arithmetic mean reaches <strong>0.782 AUROC</strong>, exceeding every individual factor. Removing any of the four factor groups lowers AUROC and AP.</p>
    <p class="study-context"><a href="https://github.com/AIR-THU/DAIR-V2X-Seq/tree/main/dataset/v2x-seq-spd">V2X-Seq-SPD</a> + <a href="https://mobility-lab.seas.ucla.edu/v2x-real/">V2X-Real</a> · vehicle and roadside agents · 175,526 detections · 5,415 tracks</p>
    <details class="research-details">
      <summary>Trust functions, metric definitions, and uncertainty</summary>

      | Trust function | Fitted | mAP ↑ |
      | :--- | :---: | ---: |
      | Model confidence only | — | 0.647 |
      | Arithmetic mean | No | 0.708 |
      | Weighted average | Yes | 0.707 |
      | Geometric mean | No | 0.713 |
      | Weighted geometric mean | Yes | 0.718 |
      | MLP | Yes | **0.778** |

      <p><strong>mAP</strong> is the mean of average precision per predicted class for ranking correct detections; it is not an accuracy percentage. <strong>AUROC</strong> measures separation between correct and incorrect detections across score thresholds. A detection is correct when one-to-one matching gives bird's-eye-view intersection-over-union ≥ 0.3, regardless of class.</p>
      <p>Fitted components use the official training splits; results use official validation splits. The MLP uses two hidden layers of eight tanh units. Across five seeds, its mAP is 0.772 [0.690, 0.821]; the paper reports a gain over model confidence of 0.125 [0.076, 0.175]. Intervals are 95% track-bootstrap intervals.</p>
    </details>
  </article>
  <article class="result-study">
    <span class="study-label">2 / Robustness · controlled simulation</span>
    <h3>Which reports survive an attack?</h3>
    <p>Persistent and hedged ghosts reduce the attacked sender's overall fusion rate. Freshness sharply reduces admission for delayed messages. Unobservable ghosts and intermittent attacks remain harder cases.</p>
    <PaperResults />
    <p class="study-context">Complementary real-data replay: the honest peer passes admission in 97.2% of frames across six V2X-Seq-SPD sequences; 4.7% of its reports are withheld.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="result-study">
    <span class="study-label">3 / Runtime · desktop and embedded hardware</span>
    <h3>What is the processing cost?</h3>
    <p>The local chain has a median latency of <strong>18.0 ms</strong> on the workstation and <strong>44.1 ms</strong> on Jetson AGX Thor. The global stage is timed separately: with five agents it takes <strong>8.9 ms</strong> and <strong>16.4 ms</strong>, respectively.</p>
    <details class="research-details">
      <summary>Median, tail latency, and scaling</summary>

      | Stage | Workstation p50 | Jetson AGX Thor p50 |
      | :--- | ---: | ---: |
      | Detection + tracking + local trust | 18.0 ms | 44.1 ms |
      | Global stage, 2 agents | 5.4 ms | 10.0 ms |
      | Global stage, 5 agents | 8.9 ms | 16.4 ms |
      | Global stage, 10 agents | 15.6 ms | 29.9 ms |
      | Global stage, 20 agents | 33.3 ms | 66.2 ms |

      <p><strong>p50</strong> is the median: half of completed frames finish faster and half slower. Local-chain p95 is 30.8 ms on the workstation and 73.4 ms on Thor. Thor completes 95.2% of replayed validation frames; the workstation completes all of them. This is not an end-to-end 50 ms guarantee.</p>
      <p>The workstation uses an AMD Ryzen 9 9900X and NVIDIA RTX 5090. Local timing includes DDS transport at 10 Hz recording speed; global benchmarks use synthetic scenes with known associations, 32 objects per agent, and a 20 Hz flush rate.</p>
    </details>
  </article>
</section>

<section id="limitations" class="review-section">
  <p class="eyebrow">05 / Scope</p>
  <h2>Evidence has limits. So does trust.</h2>
  <div class="scope-grid">
    <div><h3>What remains difficult</h3><p>Ghosts outside ego's evidence have 99.5% overall sender-report fusion in simulation. Intermittent attacks retain 87.0%. Absence of corroboration cannot establish a contradiction. Learned local scores also transfer imperfectly between vehicle and roadside agents.</p></div>
    <div><h3>What the system assumes</h3><p>Authenticated identities, trustworthy timestamps, correct ego localization, and stable track identities. Secure identity continuity, stronger unseen-report verdicts, and improved calibration remain future work.</p></div>
  </div>
</section>
