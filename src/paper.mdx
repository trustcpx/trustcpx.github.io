### Abstract

Cooperative perception (CP) approaches to autonomous driving position themselves as a natural successor to single-vehicle solutions that often suffer from occlusion, a common precursor to collisions and a particular problem at intersections. However, the incorporation of external sensor data (from other vehicles, roadside units) hinges upon trust – if the ego vehicle cannot deduce which data is truthful, timely, and accurate, it leaves itself vulnerable to faulty or adversarial agents. Existing CP approaches largely focus on tackling one or two facets of trust in isolation (such as on-off attacks, Sybil attacks, or data spoofing), and, to our knowledge, no end-to-end pipeline exists to address the full range of threats. We adapt and extend reputation methods from prior work to build such a pipeline and test it on real-world datasets. Each vehicle maintains a local estimate of every peer, updated every iteration based on message freshness, reputation volatility, and accumulated agreement with independently observed evidence. Furthermore, accumulated agreement itself is reputation-weighted, so low-standing senders cannot vouch for one another. The final fusion is gated upon reputation, so only trustworthy objects are passed for downstream tasks such as trajectory planning. Evaluated on real-world data recorded at a smart intersection, the pipeline holds per-frame latency under the 50 ms real-time budget on the target edge device, and has been demonstrated to industry and transportation partners, with live field deployment planned next.

</HighlightedSection>

## Overview

Connected vehicles can extend their field of view through cooperative perception, but shared detections are useful only when the receiver can assess their provenance and reliability. TrustCPX is a decentralized, vehicle-local trust layer that evaluates every peer before its objects enter fusion. It is designed for the heterogeneous, delayed, and potentially adversarial messages encountered by real V2X systems [@chen2019cooper; @v2xreal2023].

For each received message, TrustCPX combines freshness, reputation volatility, and agreement with independently observed evidence. Agreement is itself reputation-weighted, limiting collusion by low-standing peers. The resulting local reputation gates fusion, preserving actionable detections while excluding sources that cannot be trusted.

## Trust-aware fusion

The pipeline is lightweight enough to run online: messages are scored at every iteration, peer estimates are updated locally, and fusion is conditioned on reputation. This retains the benefits of multi-agent perception while reducing the effect of stale, inconsistent, or malicious reports. Our evaluation uses real-world cooperative driving data from a smart intersection, represented in the V2X-Real dataset [@v2xreal2023], and BEV detections produced with a multimodal fusion backbone [@liu2022bevfusion].
